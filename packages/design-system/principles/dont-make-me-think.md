# Don't Make Me Think in this product

Our users are community organisers, researchers, artists and funders, many on phones and weak connections, often busy and sometimes unsure whether this is for them. Krug's concern is that they should not have to work to understand us.

## Arriving

- **Home answers the newcomer's questions on one phone screen:** what this is, what is here, what I can do, why here, where to start (119, 128-129). Show a plain tagline of six to eight words beside the name, a start action, a way to search and one sign of life, such as funds closing soon (125-127, 115). A short verb like Weave cannot do this alone (32).
- **People also land deep.** Links from email and social media open the exact record and orient the visitor from there (121-122, 185-186).
- **Protect Home.** Partnerships, places, updates and lenses will all want space. Each addition displaces something, so keep a priority list (129-130).

## Finding the way

- **Every page names itself,** echoing the label that led there. Product name top-left returns home. Mark the current section with at least two cues (92-113).
- **Search is a box, a button and the word Search.** Scope options appear only on results (102-103). Lists show their size (91), and visited items look different (237-238).
- **Form pages trim navigation** down to the name, home and helpful status (97).
- **Run the trunk test on random pages:** open a deep link cold at 360 px, hold it at arm's length, and find the product name, the page name, the main areas, your location and search within ten seconds (112-113). Repeat in installed-app mode, where the back button may be missing.

## Choosing

- **Make the first plausible tap correct and undoing cheap,** because people scan and take the first reasonable option (39-47). Cards open with their reason, so that first impression is useful. Label steward-picked items apart from ranked ones (36).
- **Options must not overlap.** Sign in, create account and join are three doors to one room. Keep one (70-73).
- **Labels predict what happens** (70). Ask for an introduction says what is sent and what follows.
- **Help is brief and timely,** right where the hard question is (75).

## Reading

- **Treat each element as noise until it earns its place** (62-63).
- **Cut about half the words, then half again.** Remove welcome text and instructions the design can replace (77-80).
- **Format for scanning:** short paragraphs, headings closer to what they introduce, lists for parallel items (63-68). Screen-reader users scan by ear, so put meaning first (219-220).

## Conversation and forms

The three-step conversation risks hiding structure, so always show a step map and the record so far (91-93). Each prompt is one sentence, as a visible label (75, 237). Accept data the way people type it: a place field suggests completions and never rejects an abbreviation (34-36, 207). Ask for eligibility facts only when a fund needs them (207-208).

## Goodwill and honesty

- **Goodwill is limited.** It depends on the person and the moment. Hiding things, asking for more than the task needs, and sloppy errors drain it. Saving steps and owning mistakes refills it (204-211).
- **Volunteer unwelcome facts:** eligibility limits, stale data, what is stored (209).
- **Errors say what happened and keep what was typed** (210-211).
- **Persuade honestly.** No pre-checked or half-hidden opt-ins (231-233). The saving flow in [08-accounts-and-saving.md](../../../docs/platform/08-accounts-and-saving.md) comes directly from this chapter.

## Accessibility

Fix what confuses everyone first, then the specifics: labelled fields, heading order, keyboard access, a skip link, strong contrast and text that can grow (212-222).

## Testing

Reactions to a design are personal, so end taste arguments by asking whether this element, in these words, works for likely users, and then go and watch them (132-140). See the [testing plan](usability-testing.md).

## Where we go past the book

The book is from 2014 and does not cover passwordless codes, installable apps, voice input or AI assistants. Its advice on saving is inference from its goodwill and mobile chapters. We treat all of it as hypotheses to test.
