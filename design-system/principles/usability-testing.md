# Usability testing plan

Phases 0 and 1 run no third-party analytics, so watching people is our main feedback. This plan follows chapter 9 and chapter 13 of Krug's book (141-172, 225-226). Three people find most of the serious problems, and testing early with something rough beats testing late with something polished.

## Rhythm

- A fixed slot every second week until Phase 1 ends, then monthly: half a day, three participants, same-day debrief.
- Before the first public demo, run two sessions on anything clickable. If nothing is clickable yet, use the current Funding Commons site and the shared partnerships document as a baseline.
- Sketches count. Don't wait for a build.

## Participants

Three per round: two from the target group and one outsider. Rotate across community groups, researchers and builders, artists and funders. Include people who only use phones and people on weak connections.

Recruit through node stewards, the initiatives in the partnerships document, group chats, council calls and demo attendees. Offer a slightly generous honorarium, or a gift to their initiative. Run sessions remotely. Ask them to share their phone screen, or point a camera at fingers and screen. Use their own phone and mail app.

## Tasks

Pick three or four per session. Give one card at a time.

1. **Name test.** Which tap would find people to work with? Money closing soon? Needs in your watershed?
2. **Home tour.** Say what this is, before tapping anything.
3. **Describe your project.** Type, speak or paste a link. Name one need and one thing you could offer.
4. **Keep your work.** You have to stop now and continue tomorrow on another device. Do what you would do. This covers the mail-app round trip and airplane mode.
5. **Act.** Pick the partner and the fund you would act on first, and say why.
6. **Join in.** Find whether anyone is already forming an application for a fund, and how you would join them.
7. **Your place.** Find your area and what it needs. Find funders that could suit you, and say what you would check first.
8. **Weekly update.** Open the sample email, confirm one record, then stop the emails.

## Script

- You are helping us check the app. You are not being assessed, and no move is a mistake.
- Please say what you notice, expect and wonder as you go. I will mostly stay quiet and answer questions at the end. If you get stuck, carry on as you would alone.
- You can stop at any time. With your permission I record the screen and voice for our team only, and delete the recording after review.
- To warm up, tell me about a project you care about, and how you look for partners or funding today.
- If they go quiet, ask what is going through their mind.
- At the end, ask what confused them, what they expected and what they would tell a friend this is.

## Debrief

Hold it the same day, with council members and anyone else who watched.

1. Each observer lists the three worst problems they saw per session. Only list what was observed.
2. Pool them without debate, tally and rank the top ten.
3. Assign fixes until capacity is full, then stop. Keep a separate list of one-hour fixes.
4. Prefer removing to adding. Ignore stumbles people corrected themselves, and treat feature requests as noise.
5. Log people's own words for needs, offers and places, and take labels from them.

## What we are testing first

- Whether people understand what the product is from its name and tagline.
- Whether "initiative", "offer", the kinds of support and "partnership" make sense, or need the alternatives in [VOICE.md](../brand/VOICE.md#plain-names).
- Whether people save, and when. Measure time from tap to saved on a phone, and who returns after choosing Not now. Do not measure accounts created.
- Whether people trust the difference between pencil and ink.
