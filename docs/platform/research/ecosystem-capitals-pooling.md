## A. Forms of capital

### A1. The Eight Forms of Capital (Roland & Landua)

**Provenance.** The framework was written by Ethan Roland (now publishing as Ethan Soloviev) and Gregory Landua of AppleSeed Permaculture; the canonical text lives at https://appleseedpermaculture.com/8-forms-of-capital/ and the magazine layout is hosted as a PDF whose running header reads "www.permaculture.co.uk permaculture No. 68 Eight Forms of Capital" (http://appleseedpermaculture.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/8_Forms_of_Capital_PM68.pdf). Two secondary sources place the article in *Permaculture Magazine* issue 68, 2011 (https://thegoodliferevival.com/podcast/17; https://www.unturf.com/eight-forms-of-capital/), and a 2014 Permaculture Podcast page dates the original to 2011 (https://www.thepermaculturepodcast.com/2014/8forms/). The book-length expansion is *Regenerative Enterprise: Optimizing for Multi-capital Abundance* by Gregory Landua and Ethan Roland (2013; self-published via Lulu: https://www.lulu.com/shop/gregory-landua-and-ethan-roland/regenerative-enterprise-optimizing-for-multi-capital-abundance/ebook/product-16r59pmw.html). Landua later co-founded Regen Network (https://registry.regen.network/team/gregory-landua).

**Verbatim definitions.** The canonical text is republished by co-author Ethan Soloviev under the copyright line "Ethan Roland Soloviev & Gregory Landua" (2011) at https://ethansoloviev.com/8-forms-of-capital/; quotes below are from that page (the AppleSeed original could not be fetched because of a self-signed TLS certificate, and the P2P Foundation mirror returned HTTP 403):

| Capital | Original definition (quoted) | Currency / examples named in the article |
|---|---|---|
| Social | "Influence and connections are social capital." | favours or decision-making influence that one party can "owe" another |
| Material | "Non-living physical objects form material capital." | "stone, metal, timber, and fossil fuels", built structures and tools; "copper or steel" can serve as a medium of exchange |
| Financial | "Money, currencies, securities and other instruments of the global financial system." | "US dollars"; precious metals are also filed here |
| Living | "Living capital is made up of the animals, plants, water and soil of our land" | measured (after Catherine Austin Fitts) in "ounces, acres, and hooves"; "Natural Capital" noted as a possible synonym |
| Intellectual | "Intellectual capital is best described as a 'knowledge' asset." | university study framed as money exchanged for knowledge |
| Experiential (or Human) | "We accumulate experiential capital through actually organizing a project in our community" | "building a strawbale house", "completing a permaculture design" |
| Spiritual | built through faith, spiritual practice or any path connecting a person with self and cosmos; "deeper, more personal and less quantifiable" than intellectual or experiential capital | karma as a "spiritual currency"; the Tzutujil Maya notion of a "spiritual debt" owed to existence |
| Cultural | "cultural capital can only be gathered by a community of people." | creative works, shared songs, harvest or holiday gatherings |

The article defines currency as "the generally accepted (or in use) medium of exchange between pools of capital", notes that sometimes "the currency is the capital itself", and shows the per-form currency mapping only as an image titled "Eight Forms of Currency" (https://ethansoloviev.com/8-forms-of-capital/). The ServiceSpace repost (10 Dec 2013) lists the eight in the order spiritual, social, material, financial, living (natural), cultural, experiential, intellectual and likewise carries the currency list only as an image (https://www.servicespace.org/blog/view.php?id=14043).

**Pools, flows, "complexed".** The article treats pools as the stores of each capital and flows as movements between them; it says local economies are "more defined by the flows of money rather than by the pools", and distinguishes "Intra-capital flows, between the same type of capital" (dollars spent on a bond; tomato seeds traded for eggs) from "Inter-capital flows, between different types of capital" (paying a master builder for an apprenticeship turns money into skills, knowledge and social ties). Resources are "'complexed' with each other to create more sophisticated materials or structures", and currencies can be "complexed" into "more interconnected and functional forms" (https://ethansoloviev.com/8-forms-of-capital/). The term "nurture capital" does not appear in the article, and the web version cites Sun Magazine (Dec 2009, Prechtel) and Mollison (1988) rather than Permaculture Magazine; the PM68 PDF is the magazine edition (http://appleseedpermaculture.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/04/8_Forms_of_Capital_PM68.pdf).

Secondary sources that corroborate the list and ordering: the Permaculture Podcast episode outline names the eight as financial, living, material, social, cultural, intellectual, spiritual and experiential (https://www.thepermaculturepodcast.com/2014/8forms/); the Open Source Ecology wiki links the original and proposes a derived "9 Forms of Capital" (https://wiki.opensourceecology.org/wiki/8_Forms_of_Capital); a reader comment on the Resilience.org repost proposes "Health & Well being Capital" as a ninth (https://www.resilience.org/?p=2251776).

**Pools, flows and "complex".** In the 2014 Peak Prosperity interview reposted by Resilience.org, Roland calls living capital "the foundational pool", says "we need flow in all these different forms of capital", and frames the aim as "flow financial capital into living capital"; he also describes "whole enterprise ecologies or enterprise ecosystems" and "more complex and healthy multi-capital profits", and defines cultural capital as "the ability of a group of people to step into ceremony together" (https://www.resilience.org/?p=2251776). The P2P Foundation summary of the book says Roland and Landua rank living and cultural capital as the most promising levers for systemic change (https://www.thepermaculturepodcast.com/2014/8forms/). **[Inference]** The "eight forms of capital complex" wording the brief asks about does not appear in any source I could fetch; the closest documented concepts are "pools" (stocks), "flows" (exchanges between forms) and "enterprise ecologies/ecosystems".

**Nurture capital.** The book defines a regenerative enterprise as one that actively strengthens shared pools of social, cultural, spiritual and living capital, and argues a single company cannot do this alone, so it must join symbiotic networks it calls "Regenerative Enterprise Ecosystems" (summary at https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Regenerative_Enterprise). Daniel Christian Wahl's commentary on the book uses "nurture capitals" for exactly those four forms, social, living, spiritual and cultural (https://readmedium.com/co-creating-regenerative-enterprises-20d9a3bc1c97). A Permaculture Voices episode page describes the model as tracking "the movement and stock of all eight" capitals (https://permaculturevoices.libsyn.com/webpage/2016/10/07). Note the unrelated use of "nurture capital" by Woody Tasch/Slow Money (https://slowmoney.org/books/soil-theory-and-practice-of-nurture-capital/); do not conflate.

**[Inference for the PRD]** The book's framing gives a ready-made data model: each initiative has *stocks* (what it holds in each capital), *flows* (offers = outbound flows it can give; needs = inbound flows it seeks), and the four nurture capitals are the ones most under-served by financial-only funder directories. "Pooling" in the Regenerative Enterprise sense is about shared pools of nurture capital inside an enterprise ecology, which maps cleanly onto the later Grassroots Economics notion of a commitment pool (Track B).

### A2. How practitioners operationalise needs/offers by capital (3–5 examples)

1. **Offers and Needs Market (Post Growth Institute)** – uses six categories rather than the eight capitals: participants "identify and exchange" their "passions, knowledge, skills, resources, opportunities, and needs" (https://offersandneeds.com/). **[Inference]** These map roughly onto experiential/spiritual (passions), intellectual (knowledge), experiential (skills), material/financial/living (resources) and social (opportunities). Details in A3.
2. **Hylo (Terran Collective)** – implements offers and needs as first-class post types ("Request", "Offer", "Resource") but does not tag them by capital form; see A4. A Notion explainer describes "discussions, requests, offers, resources, member directories, direct messaging, and chat" and ties sharing to "location, skills and interests" (https://tijnt.notion.site/Learn-more-about-Hylo-269ba552a9674e6dbe9ab84a58644beb).
3. **Murmurations "Offers & Wants" schema** – a machine-readable schema for regenerative-economy offers/wants with `exchange_type` (offer/want), `item_type` (good/service), `transaction_type` (borrow-lend, rent-lease, buy-sell, receive-donate), `geographic_scope`, `tags`, `geolocation`, `expires_at` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MurmurationsNetwork/MurmurationsLibrary/main/schemas/offers_wants_schema-v0.1.0.json). Again not capital-typed; see Track C.
4. **Gitcoin research, "The Eight Forms of Capital: Beyond Financial Metrics in Public Goods"** – applies the framework to public-goods funding and argues community identity underpins that work (https://gitcoin.co/research/eight-forms-of-capital-beyond-financial-metrics) **[unverified fetch; summary from search excerpt]**.
5. **Membership/network practitioners** – Sociocracy For All asks its members "what form of capital is SoFA providing to you" using the eight forms (https://forums.sociocracyforall.org/t/from-permaculture-8-forms-of-capital-and-what-form-of-capital-is-sofa-providing-to-you-as-a-member/955); the consultancy Think Differently runs a free "Eight forms of capital" webinar on how non-monetary capitals "build holistic wealth" (https://luma.com/fwckg9ix); a commercial "8 Forms of Capital" product recommends community exchange platforms handling barter, time-banking and community currencies alongside money (https://ocdbizsolutions.com/products/8-forms-of-capital).

**Negative findings (important for positioning).** I found no evidence that Kinship Earth, Terran Collective/Hylo, Regen Network or the Post Growth Institute tag offers or needs by the eight capitals. Kinship Earth's model is "flow funding" (no-strings grants routed to coalitions of local leaders) rather than capital tagging (https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Bioregional_Flow_Funding; https://cascadiabioregion.org/department-of-bioregion/highlights-from-the-2025-biofi-conference). Terran Collective lists "gift economics" among the practices it tests (https://p2pfoundation.net/Terran_Collective). Regen Network's connection to the framework is through co-author Landua (https://registry.regen.network/team/gregory-landua), not through a product feature. **[Inference]** A platform that lets initiatives tag each offer/need with one or more of the eight capitals (plus the OANM six as a friendlier UI layer) would be the first such tool I could identify in this space.

### A3. Offers and Needs Market (OANM): format, rounds, categories, software

- **Origin and claim.** "Developed by the PGI in 2011"; "a community process designed to facilitate inclusivity and rapid connections, even between strangers"; "typically takes the form of a 2-hour, guided event (online or in person)"; usable in "neighborhoods, workplaces, conferences, schools, cohorts"; runnable by "anyone with basic training" (https://postgrowth.org/v2/the-offers-and-needs-market/). PGI's homepage describes it as a 90-minute process (https://postgrowth.org/), and a Compassionate Austin listing also says 90 minutes (https://sage-knox-a85h.squarespace.com/offers-needs-market); the dedicated site says two hours (https://offersandneeds.com/). Donnie Maclurcan (PGI) is named as creator in a Shareable training listing (https://www.shareable.net/events/offers-and-needs-market-facilitators-training-online/).
- **Categories.** Participants "identify and exchange" their "passions, knowledge, skills, resources, opportunities, and needs" (https://offersandneeds.com/). Each exchange is arranged by the individuals involved as "money, barter, bargaining, or a gift" (https://postgrowth.org/v2/the-offers-and-needs-market/), and participants can state when the exchange is possible, how urgent it is, where it would happen, and whether it is paid, traded, negotiated or freely given (https://medium.com/postgrowth/learning-to-share-offers-and-needs-b45c689880f4) **[unverified fetch; from search excerpt]**.
- **Round structure (observed agenda, Grand Rapids OANM, 21 Oct 2025, 2 hours):** (1) Introduction; (2) Offers round, guided with reflection and networking, in groups of 6–8; (3) 5-minute break; (4) Needs round, same format, groups of 6–8; (5) a combined round blending offers and needs in different small groups; (6) closing reflections and a feedback questionnaire (https://www.meetup.com/offers-and-needs-market-oanm/events/310425674). Participants rotate through small groups; no result uses the word "rounds" formally, and no automated matching is described.
- **Facilitation and licensing.** Basic Facilitator Training opens with a live two-hour cohort session; Advanced Facilitator Training runs 10 hours as five weekly Zoom sessions; a Facilitator Certification path exists; an in-person training was held in Medford, Oregon, 6–8 March 2026 at USD 400 (https://offersandneeds.com/training-1/bft/; https://offersandneeds.com/training-1/aft/; https://offersandneeds.com/events/the-offers-and-needs-market-facilitator-training-march-2026/2026-03-06/). The Kola Nut Collaborative ran a call for an OANM Facilitator Network (https://kolanutcollab.org/portfolio-item/oanm-fn/). Documented deployments include Zimbabwe (IGNITE Youth), Chicago (Worker Co-op Week), MAYMA (Latin America, online), a Scottish intentional community, a cleaning co-op, PhD students and an Alabama agricultural community (https://offersandneeds.com/); a 2025 Mutual Aid Oregon programme (https://offersandneeds.com/mutual-aid-oregon-2025/).
- **Software.** No OANM app was found. Materials listed are "OANM Event Surveys" and "The OANM Toolkit"; virtual sessions run on Zoom (https://offersandneeds.com/; https://offersandneeds.com/training-1/aft/). The impact page lists 14 self-reported outcome measures (confidence voicing needs, meeting collaborators, starting/growing a business, volunteering, local sourcing, trust) but published no counts (https://offersandneeds.com/impact).
- **[Inference]** OANM is a facilitation protocol, not software. A digital needs/offers directory could serve as the persistent "after-market" layer: capture what was voiced in rounds, keep it searchable, and record exchange terms (gift / barter / negotiated / paid) using OANM's own vocabulary.

### A4. Hylo as a reference implementation of offers/requests

- Post types in the user guide: Discussion, Request, Offer, Resource, Project, plus Event in the manual; requests, offers and resources can carry timeframes and be flagged as unavailable or no longer needed; projects allow invitations and donation/project-management links; a Public Map plots public groups and posts (https://hylozoic.gitbook.io/hylo/guides/hylo-user-guide/posts/types-of-posts; https://hylozoic.gitbook.io/hylo/hylo-user-manual/posts). The hylo.com About page additionally shows a "Proposal" type in its sample group (https://hylo.com/about).
- Governance/organisation: maintained by Terran Collective (Bay Area), sociocracy with five circles, fiscally sponsored (Empowerment Works), "well over 100 active groups" / "hundreds" of groups, began 2012, named a 2026 NTEN Pizzigati Prize winner, code "open and licensed under Apache 2.0" at github.com/hylozoic/hylo (https://hylo.com/about; https://hylozoic.gitbook.io/hylo/about/faq). Holo (Holochain) acquired Hylo in 2019 and Terran took stewardship in 2020 (https://medium.com/terran-collective/in-partnership-with-holo-terran-collective-is-building-hylo-the-collaboration-platform-for-a-224dd6d1be1e).
- Bioregional vision: Hylo treats bioregional boundaries as subjective and overlapping, offers default regions by location while letting users outline their own, and plans to extend governance and finance tools to bioregional groups with a pilot in one region (https://hylozoic.gitbook.io/hylo/about/bioregional-vision). Planet Drum notes Hylo "emerged from the Shasta bioregion" (https://planetdrum.org/hylo/). BioFi ran a "Hylo for Bioregional Organizing Teams" deep dive (https://luma.com/e8fdtabl).
- API: an early-stage partner API; access by emailing hello@hylo.com for a client_id/secret; OAuth 2.0 + OpenID Connect (Authorization Code with PKCE at https://hylo.com/noo/oauth/auth and token at https://hylo.com/noo/oauth/token; Client Credentials with `api:read`/`api:write` scopes, 2-hour tokens); GraphQL at https://hylo.com/noo/graphql; schema in the Hylozoic/hylo monorepo (apps/backend/api/graphql/schema.graphql) (https://hylozoic.gitbook.io/hylo/guides/api-documentation). The older API repo hylo-node ("The Hylo API", ~5,030 commits) remains public (https://github.com/Hylozoic/hylo-node).

## B. Commitment pooling, Grassroots Economics, CLC and cosmo-localism

### B1. The protocol and its definitions

**Papers.** The founding paper is William O. Ruddick, "Letters from the Field: Commitment Pooling – An Economic Protocol Inspired by Ancestral Wisdom", *International Journal of Community Currency Research* Vol. 27 (2023), pp. 54–79, published 30 June 2023, DOI 10.15133/j.ijccr.2023.004; its abstract calls it a "prospective report" on pooled commitments to "curate community resources and exchange them fairly", rooted in "mutual service" traditions and aimed at "autonomous, decentralized, non-monetary, and polycentric systems" (https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9297). The P2P Foundation wiki files it under 2024 and Resilience.org treated a March 2024 working paper, "Commitment Pooling: A Protocol for Grassroots Economics", as new (https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Commitment_Pooling_as_an_Economic_Protocol_Insprired_by_Ancestral_Wisdom; https://www.resilience.org/?p=3500114), so "2024 paper" in the brief most likely refers to that working-paper circulation of the 2023 article. The follow-up is Ruddick, "Proto-Social Infrastructure and Stewardship of Commitment Pooling", IJCCR Vol. 30 No. 1 (2026), Special Issue Rome, published 28 March 2026, keywords "Proto-social, infrastructure, resource coordination, stewardship, mycorrhizal, economic commons, blockchain"; abstract: "Commitment Pooling as a commons-based resource coordination framework", whose protocol "establishes rules for the exchange of resources, emphasizing reciprocity and mutual support", "blending proto-social systems like rotational labor with modern technologies such as blockchain", with stewardship "central to this framework" (https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9512). Its DOI is given as 10.26034/zh.ijccr.2026.9512 on the P2P wiki summary (https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Stewardship_of_Commitment_Pooling) **[search excerpt]**.

**Four interfaces / four concepts.** Grassroots Economics (GE) describes the protocol as resting on four interfaces, Curation of Assets, Limiter, Price Index and Exchange Logic, where the Price Index "applies a relative value approach" to set each commitment's worth (https://grassrootseconomics.org/pages/how-it-works) **[search excerpt]**. The current Cosmo-Local Credit protocol reference (Protocol v1.1.0) groups pool functions into four concepts: **Curation**, "admit supported tokens or vouchers"; **Valuation**, "publish the method used for an exchange rate or quote"; **Limitation**, "apply current Pool token-balance caps or other separately implemented controls"; **Exchange**, "hold inventory, execute swaps, account for fees, and emit transaction records" (https://docs-4qq.pages.dev/white-paper/chapter-01-commitment-pooling-protocol-cpp-the-core-primitive, a preview build of the docs.cosmolocal.credit white paper).

**Core definitions (same source plus the CLC docs at https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/llms-full.txt):**
- Commitment: "an identified party's promise of future delivery" (examples: "food, transport, labor, storage", or any lawful good, service, benefit or performance). The docs also speak of a "commons of commitments", i.e. "promises that can be exchanged, fulfilled, and renewed without requiring one central authority".
- Voucher: "a token or record represented as that commitment under published terms" / "a token or record that an issuer represents as a redeemable commitment under published terms". Voucher terms identify "the issuer, Offering, capacity, place, timing, restrictions", plus presentment, fulfilment, complaints and discharge. Ordinary sends, deposits and swaps are "not automatically a loan or repayment".
- Offering: "catalog record for goods or services associated with a voucher".
- Commitment Pool: "the governed arrangement" / "a governed arrangement that curates assets, publishes rules, and enables exchange"; pools take in vouchers, set exchange rules, hold stock and allow swaps; "Pool exchange and issuer fulfillment are separate lifecycles".
- Pool Steward: "publishes and administers Pool rules and any expressly assumed guarantee"; may be a person, co-op, public body, federation or multisig. Other roles: Pool owner, proxy administrator, dependency controllers, route finder/operator, guarantor ("assumes a defined obligation only through published, funded terms").
- Exchange rules (direct swap): registry lookup → input measurement → quote (configured quoter or raw-unit parity) → limiter test → fee calculation → output-stock check → transfers of fee and output → event logging; a separate router only computes quotes across several pools. The docs stress that "On-chain records still do not prove real-world fulfillment or social impact."
- Relative value index: the PriceIndexQuoter "supplies the relative prices used to quote swaps", using 4 decimal places of fixed-point precision, so the default 10_000 "corresponds to 10 KES of commodities" (illustrative USD token 145_000, TZS 50) (https://software.grassrootseconomics.org/smart-contracts/swappool). In v1.1 the RelativeQuoter adds "owner-managed relative price indexes" to decimal normalisation and "An unset token index defaults to parity" (https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/llms-full.txt).
- Curation and limiter mechanics: the TokenRegistry is an allowlist and "Pools reject any asset that isn't listed"; the Limiter sets a maximum per deposit per token/pool pair via setLimitFor; the v1.1 Limiter "caps a token balance at a Pool" and "does not provide rolling, per-account, or network-wide swap limits" (https://software.grassrootseconomics.org/smart-contracts/swappool; https://docs-4qq.pages.dev/white-paper/chapter-01-commitment-pooling-protocol-cpp-the-core-primitive).

### B2. Sarafu Network and the software stack

- **Chain and scale.** Sarafu moved to xDai in January 2020 (https://grassecon.substack.com/p/circling-back) and to Celo in July 2023 (https://grassecon.net/impacts-2024). GE's commitment-pooling page reports 188 Active Commitment Pools, 285,197 peer-to-peer exchanges and 745 "Active Vouchers on Celo", with a chart of "Historical Sarafu Network activity on Celo, 5 July 2023–20 July 2025" sourced from https://dune.com/grassrootseconomics/sarafu-network (https://grassrootseconomics.org/commitment-pooling). The white paper executive summary gives 188 active pools and $320,692 swap volume as of 20 July 2025 (https://docs-4qq.pages.dev/white-paper/executive-summary) **[search excerpt]**. The 2024 impact report counts 485 pool swaps and 120 pool deposits, 101+ partner groups and 3,000+ users across 33 pools (https://grassecon.net/impacts-2024).
- **Smart contracts.** The swap pool is "a set of access-controlled contracts": SwapPool (holds assets, executes swaps; ERC-20 name/symbol/decimals so a TokenRegistry can list multiple pools), TokenRegistry (allowlist), Limiter (optional TokenLimiter), PriceIndexQuoter. Deployment uses the ge-publish CLI and at least one CELO for gas in the order token-registry → limiter → swap-pool → price-index-quoter; configuration via setQuoter, setFee (PPM; 5000 = 0.5%), addWriter/add, setLimitFor, setPriceIndexValue; deposit is one-way ("nothing comes back"), and withdraw(wantedToken, depositedToken, amount) performs the swap; error codes ERR_REGISTRY, ERR_UNAUTH_TOKEN, ERR_LIMITER, ERR_QUOTER etc. Source: https://github.com/grassrootseconomics/erc20-pool ("Permissioned ERC20 swap pool for EVM", fork of nolash/erc20-pool, Solidity + Python, "satisfies the CIC TokenSwap interface"); CLI: https://github.com/grassrootseconomics/ge-publish; docs licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://software.grassrootseconomics.org/smart-contracts/swappool). The v1.1.0 CLC contract family is GiftableToken, SwapPool, DecimalQuoter, RelativeQuoter, OracleQuoter, OracleRelay, FeePolicy, Limiter, ProtocolFeeController, TokenUniqueSymbolIndex, AccountsIndex, ContractRegistry, CAT, SwapRouter, Splitter, EthFaucet, PeriodSimple and RescueVault, deployed as proxies via Solady's ERC1967Factory; integrators are told to prefer the bounded six-argument `withdraw` overload with a minimum-output check and a deadline (https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/llms-full.txt).
- **Repositories and licences.** The GitHub organisation (111 repos) states its software is AGPL 3.0 and lists, among others: sarafu.network ("Sarafu Network dApp", TypeScript, AGPL-3.0, updated 2026-10-12), eth-custodial ("EOA key management and EVM signing API service", Go), sarafu-vise (USSD service "built using the go-vise engine", run targets CLI/Africastalking/Async/Http, AGPL-3.0), sarafu-api, ge-publish, eth-indexer, eth-tracker, ethutils ("High level Go wrapper for interacting with EVM chains"), cel2-node, plus games "recipes" (cook-and-trade with vouchers) and "guild" (https://github.com/grassrootseconomics; https://github.com/grassrootseconomics/sarafu-vise; https://github.com/grassrootseconomics/eth-custodial). The dApp README calls sarafu.network "the premier dApp for interacting with CAV's (Community Asset Vouchers) and Commitment Pools", built on Next.js and PostgreSQL with a "Client-side wallet interface" on Celo and support for "Paper Wallets" (https://github.com/grassrootseconomics/sarafu.network).
- **Wallet model (do participants need wallets?).** Today, yes for the dApp: it relies on external wallets, which the team itself says "makes it difficult for many people" (naming Valora, MetaMask, Rabby). On 23 September 2025 Mohamed Sohail (GE Engineering & Governance) announced Sarafu Wallet, "a fully open source, non custodial and cross platform wallet" under AGPL-3.0, Celo-only, designed around EIP-7702 with account/fee abstraction, sponsored gas, ENS community subdomains, optional passkey/biometric login and access channels "USSD (interoperable) /QR/NFC"; a founder reply notes earlier USSD services used custodial accounts and the goal is USSD menus without custody; a "Sarafu Voting Token" governing a "Protocol Pool" is proposed; a 11 Oct 2025 reply says "offramping is already shipped" (https://forum.celo.org/t/introducing-the-development-of-sarafu-wallet-a-public-good-wallet-for-celo/12402). The CLC docs say "Use the CLC App on a phone or computer", that "QR codes and printable paper wallets provide additional ways to take part", that registration currently requires "A Wallet address, authentication proof, name, and email", that passkeys are supported as authentication data, and that GEF "may be unable to recover a lost wallet" and is not a custodian unless it expressly accepts that role (https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/; https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/llms-full.txt). The legacy custodial USSD service was reachable at *384*96# (Safaricom) and *483*46# (Airtel) with a Send / My Sarafu / My Account / Help menu (https://bitcoinke.io/?p=21365, undated, Covid-era). In 2025 GE added a "Swap option" to the USSD menu (Q2), "Send 'Mpesa'" via a Pretium integration (Q3) and a "Credit – Send" option (Q4) (https://grassecon.substack.com/p/2025-harvest). No Telegram interface was found.
- **APIs/SDKs.** No public REST/GraphQL SDK is documented; integration is via the contracts and the ge-publish CLI (https://software.grassrootseconomics.org/smart-contracts/swappool). eth-custodial exposes a signing API, ethutils is a Go client library, and the CLC backend clc-core is described as "Deployer, route solver, relayer and custodial key management backend" (https://github.com/grassrootseconomics; https://github.com/cosmo-local-credit). **[Inference]** A third-party platform integrates by (a) reading Celo events via eth-indexer/eth-tracker or Dune, and (b) submitting transactions through viem/wagmi against the published contracts, or by running clc-core/eth-custodial for custodial signing.

### B6. Design pattern: a needs/offers directory feeding a commitment pool without web3 wallets at first

**[Inference, grounded in the sources above.]** CLC already separates three objects the directory can mirror: an *Offering* (catalogue record of goods/services), a *voucher* (a commitment under published terms, identifying "issuer, Offering, capacity, place, timing, restrictions") and a *Pool* (curation, valuation, limitation, exchange). GE's own history ran paper → mobile/USSD with custodial accounts → blockchain, and its current stack still ships "custodial key management" (clc-core) and a signing API (eth-custodial), plus paper wallets and QR codes. That supports a staged design:

1. **Stage 0, off-chain records (no wallets).** Each initiative profile lists offers and needs tagged by the eight capitals. Any offer that is a deliverable good/service is additionally stored as an *Offering* record with the CLC voucher-term fields (unit, capacity, place, timing, restrictions, redemption and complaint terms). A *commitment* between two initiatives (e.g., "40 hours of GIS mapping for the watershed council by June") is recorded as a signed JSON document (platform-held keys; hash stored) with states proposed → accepted → fulfilled → discharged, mirroring CLC's "Pool exchange and issuer fulfillment are separate lifecycles". Publishing the offers/needs half with the Murmurations `offers_wants_schema` (exchange_type, item_type, transaction_type incl. receive-donate, geographic_scope, expires_at) keeps the directory interoperable from day one (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MurmurationsNetwork/MurmurationsLibrary/main/schemas/offers_wants_schema-v0.1.0.json).
2. **Stage 1, custodial anchoring.** A steward organisation runs clc-core/eth-custodial, creates Celo accounts keyed to each initiative's email/passkey login (the CLC registration model), mints ERC-20 vouchers (GiftableToken) for Offerings whose issuers opt in, and replays Stage-0 commitments as voucher transfers whose events carry the off-chain record hash. Members never see seed phrases; they can export a paper wallet/QR at any time, exactly as CLC offers.
3. **Stage 2, bioregional pool.** A watershed/bioregional steward deploys a SwapPool with TokenRegistry (curation = the directory's verified members), RelativeQuoter index (valuation, e.g., 10_000 = one local reference basket), Limiter caps, and fees to a community fund; the directory's needs feed becomes the pool's demand signal and its offers feed the pool's catalogue.
4. **Stage 3, self-custody and federation.** Members migrate to Sarafu Wallet / EIP-7702 or Safe accounts; pools interconnect via SwapRouter and CLC routing, and, if desired, join the Circles/Gnosis federation GE describes. Grant-style flows can be attested in parallel on Karma GAP or Hypercerts, which Regen Coordination already uses for grantee reporting (https://gov.gitcoin.co/t/gg21-community-round-proposal-regen-coordi-nation/19114).

Risks to state in the PRD: on-chain records do not prove fulfilment (CLC docs); AGPL-3.0 obligations if clc-core/clc-app code is modified and served; and the Oct 2026 CLC Terms/Data Policy, which should be read before integrating.

**White paper (added).** The CLC white paper is titled "Cosmo-Local Credit (CLC)", version 0.8, dated 30 September 2026, by William O. Ruddick and Mohamed Sohail; it restates the Sarafu-era Celo figures (5 July 2023–20 July 2025, from the Dune dashboard) as "26,367 users", "188 unique active Commitment Pools", "$320,692 Pool swap volume", "285,197 peer-to-peer exchanges", "745 unique active vouchers" and "899 reports published", with the caveat "These figures are historical evidence, not current CLC usage figures"; it defines "Cosmo-local means sharing open standards, software, and knowledge globally" while local parties keep control of issuing, fulfilling and governing; it proposes (not ships) a CLC governance token, stCLC/sCLC receipts, a timelocked governance vault, a "CLC Network Pool", "An SDK for executed routes" and "registry snapshot and export tools" in a fork kit, and moving "From isolated Pools to a federated network" (the current SwapRouter "provides quotes only"); protocol contracts in the repo's src directory are "published under AGPL-3.0" (https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/llms-full.txt).

## C. Decentralised directory interoperability and the adjacent ecosystem

### C1. Murmurations Protocol

- **What it is.** "A distributed data sharing protocol to facilitate collaboration at scale" with three parts: *Profiles* (JSON files describing one entity, hosted wherever the owner likes, whose address is submitted), the *Network* (indexes recording each profile's address and last change) and *Aggregators* (software that polls the indexes to build maps, listings and content feeds) (https://murmurations.network/). The architecture page calls nodes "data creators" and aggregators "data processors", both validating against JSON Schema; nodes "would ideally host their own data in order to retain control of it", though hosting platforms such as the Murmurations Profile Generator can do so; requirements include "Prioritize decentralized storage solutions" and "Must use open source software"; "All the software we build ourselves is open source under the GPLv3 License"; stack: Linux, Golang, JavaScript/Svelte; IPFS replication is a possible future enhancement (https://docs.murmurations.network/about/architecture.html).
- **Publishing and querying.** A node publishes its profile JSON on its own site, then POSTs to the index referencing that URL; the index fetches the profile, validates it against the schema(s) it links, and stores only a subset of fields for search; aggregators call `get_nodes` and receive matching nodes with their `profile_url`, then download the full profiles themselves. An optional `expires` field (Unix seconds) makes the index auto-mark a profile deleted. Cloudflare Bot Fight Mode can silently block the index fetch, producing "Profile Not Found - could not find or read from (invalid JSON)". The full endpoint/parameter reference is the SwaggerHub IndexAPI 2.0.0 spec (https://docs.murmurations.network/developers/index-api.html; https://app.swaggerhub.com/apis-docs/MurmurationsNetwork/IndexAPI/2.0.0). A live query on 31 Oct 2026, `GET https://index.murmurations.network/v2/nodes?schema=organizations_schema-v1.0.0&page_size=2`, returned `meta.number_of_results` = 7,237 organisation profiles, with per-node fields `name`, `primary_url`, `profile_url`, `tags`, `locality`, `region`, `country`, `geolocation{lat,lon}`, `last_updated`, `status` and `linked_schemas`, and `links.self/next/last` pagination (https://index.murmurations.network/v2/nodes?schema=organizations_schema-v1.0.0&page_size=2).
- **Code and licences.** MurmurationsServices: "Index, Library and other microservices that implement the Murmurations protocol", Go, GPL-3.0, deployed with Rancher on Kubernetes (https://github.com/MurmurationsNetwork/MurmurationsServices). MurmurationsLibrary: "contains all Murmurations schemas and field definitions", GPL-3.0 (https://github.com/MurmurationsNetwork/MurmurationsLibrary).
- **Schema library (live, https://library.murmurations.network/v2/schemas).** `default-v2.0.0` / `default-v2.1.0` (initial validation by the index); `organizations_schema-v1.0.0`, "Group/Project/Organization Schema", "A schema to add regenerative economy Groups, Projects and Organizations to the Murmurations Index"; `people_schema-v0.1.0`, "A schema to add individuals in the regenerative economy to the Murmurations Index"; `offers_wants_schema-v0.1.0`, "A schema to add regenerative economy participants' offers and wants to the Murmurations Index"; `complementary_currencies-v2.0.0`, "A schema to map, track and compare complementary currency projects"; `karte_von_morgen-v1.0.0`, entries from Kartevonmorgen.org; `permaculture_addon_schema-v1.0.0`, an add-on to the Organizations schema. The offers/wants schema requires `linked_schemas`, `exchange_type` (offer | want), `transaction_type` (borrow-lend, rent-lease, buy-sell, receive-donate; multiple allowed), `tags`, `title`, `description`, `geographic_scope` (local, regional, national, international) and `contact_details` (email and/or contact_form), with optional `item_type` (good | service), `image`, `details_url`, `geolocation` and `expires_at` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/MurmurationsNetwork/MurmurationsLibrary/main/schemas/offers_wants_schema-v0.1.0.json).
- **Who uses it.** Full integration: Karte von Morgen, Perma Earth, Transition Network; partial integration: EcoRestoration Alliance, Co-operatives UK, Internet of Ownership; tools: WordPress plugins including an aggregator plugin, the public map at map.murmurations.network, a custom-map guide and a status page; Murmurations describes itself as "an unfunded volunteer-led project" funded by Open Collective donations and hosted by Open Web Systems (https://murmurations.network/). It shares a co-founder with the Open Credit Network (https://pat-kane-xcjl.squarespace.com/dailyalternative/2024/1/22/murmurations-network) **[search excerpt]**; it was presented at OPEN 2020 as "aggregating the decentralised commons" (https://open.coop/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/aggregating-the-decentralised-commons.html) and to Summer of Protocols (https://forum.summerofprotocols.com/t/murmurations-protocol-for-sharing-data-across-platforms-and-networks/1646). I found no evidence that Regen Network, Hylo, ReFi DAO or Greenpill publish Murmurations profiles.
- **[Inference: how to use it.]** Publish every initiative as an `organizations_schema` profile (plus `permaculture_addon` where relevant), every offer/need as an `offers_wants_schema` profile, and every funder as an `organizations_schema` profile with funder tags, hosted at stable URLs on regencommons.com; register them with the index; run our own aggregator to pull Transition Network, Perma Earth and Karte von Morgen entries into bioregion views; and propose two add-on schemas to the Library (a `forms_of_capital` tag set and a `bioregion`/watershed field), since the Library accepts schema proposals via GitHub. This gives interoperability without building a federation protocol.

### C8. Bioregional Financing Facilities (BioFi Project)

- **Definition (verbatim).** "A Bioregional Financing Facility (BFF) is a place-based institution that mobilizes and directs capital toward the regeneration of a bioregion." "It brings grants, public funding, and investment together behind a bioregion's vision for a regenerative economy, and its strategy for getting there." (https://www.biofi.earth/what-is-a-bff)
- **Four types ("templates").** Bioregional Trust: "a catalytic grant fund that builds the foundation for bioregional action"; Bioregional Venture Studio: "incubates cohorts of early-stage enterprises designed to change a system together"; Bioregional Investment Company: "places investment and blended capital into portfolios of projects and businesses through funds and bonds"; Bioregional Bank: "provides loans, credit, and technical assistance, and keeps capital circulating within the bioregion" (https://www.biofi.earth/what-is-a-bff). The ReFi Barcelona knowledge-commons notes phase them: Phase 1 = Trust (grant-making catalyst, may steward eco-credit programmes, Common Asset Trusts) and Venture Studio (nonprofit/co-op/steward-owned/DAO, feeds deal flow to the Investment Company); Phase 2 = Investment Company (Systemic Investment Funds, Bioregional Regeneration Bonds) and Bank (low-cost loans, credit lines, everyday banking, possibly a nature-linked supplementary currency) (https://publish.obsidian.md/refi-barcelona/Bioregional+Knowledge+Commons/BioFi/What+is+a+BFF%3F).
- **Functions.** "To bring financial decisions closer to place"; "To fund synergistic portfolios, not one-off deals"; "To catalyze the larger transition" (https://www.biofi.earth/what-is-a-bff); plus pooling capital from philanthropic, public, concessional and market-rate sources, decentralising decision-making, aggregating and matching projects with funders, and funding "Bioregional Hubs" and organising teams (https://publish.obsidian.md/refi-barcelona/Bioregional+Knowledge+Commons/BioFi/What+is+a+BFF%3F).
- **The report/book.** *Bioregional Financing Facilities: Reimagining Finance to Regenerate Our Planet* (2024), by Samantha Power and Leon Seefeld, with advisors including Stuart Cowan, Edward West, Indy Johar, Raj Kalia, Jan Hania, Matthew Monahan, Karl Burkart and Justin Adams, credited to the BioFi Project, Dark Matter Labs Capital Systems and the Buckminster Fuller Institute (BFI), downloadable at biofi.earth and in print (https://www.bfi.org/programs/bfi-design-lab/the-biofi-project/).
- **The organisation today.** BioFi calls itself a "think-and-do tank" for "place-based economies that regenerate", fiscally sponsored by BFI (501(c)(3)), funded by grants and consulting; work streams: BFF Design Partnerships, transition strategy and innovation (stewardship-credit frameworks, policy/legal guidance), and field building (a Community of Practice of about 1,000 people on six continents meeting in monthly "BioFi Deep Dives"; 22 teams through the BioFi Cultivator programme); team: Samantha Power (Director & Co-founder), Tyler Wakefield (Co-founder), Félix de Rosen (Research & Design), Karla Lopez; advisory circle includes Daniel Christian Wahl, John Fullerton, Matthew Monahan, Susan Witt, Edward West; BFF partners: Amazon Sacred Headwaters Alliance, Salmon Returns (Salmon Nation), Rising Tide Capital and the Schumacher Center (Hudson Valley/Berkshires), Blue Ridge Foodshed Commons; technical partners: Ma Earth, Silvi, Hypercerts, Regen Network; a "2027 Work Plan and Funding Needs" is available on request (https://www.biofi.earth/about/). Cascadia held a 2025 BioFi Conference (https://cascadiabioregion.org/department-of-bioregion/highlights-from-the-2025-biofi-conference).
- **[Inference: relation to our platform.]** The four BFF types are a funder taxonomy for the directory (grant trust, venture studio, investment company, bank/credit). Phase-1 BFFs need exactly what a needs/offers/opportunities platform produces: a curated project pipeline ("dealflow"), capacity-building needs, and matches between initiatives and funders; a Bioregional Bank's "supplementary currency" function maps onto commitment pools (Track B). Position the platform as shared infrastructure that any BFF or Bioregional Hub can adopt, rather than as a financing facility itself.
